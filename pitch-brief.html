PROOFPRINT
Swisscom · Fighting Identity Fraud in the Age of AI

A deepfake can fake your face. It can't fake your phone.

Onboarding for a Qualified Electronic Signature asks for an ID photo and a selfie. A $50 face-swap tool defeats that check today. We fuse signals a deepfake can't reproduce - device physics, not pixels - into one risk score, with a step-up only for risky sessions.

The vector

Deepfake video injected into the onboarding capture

Confirmed with Swisscom's own trust-services mentors: voice deepfakes are already covered in production. The open gap is deepfake pictures in the ID + selfie journey - exactly what current liveness checks are losing ground on industry-wide.

The mitigation

Six checks, fused into one decision

Every added check keeps genuine users passing silently. Nothing here is a bare retry - a retry only teaches an attacker which fake failed.

1–3

Identity, document, capture integrity

Face match against the deskewed document portrait · MRZ read with ICAO check digits recomputed, never trusted from OCR · virtual-camera device labels and sensor presence flagged before anything else runs.

4

Gyroscope ↔ video - the core signal

The phone tilts; a real camera's background must move with it. Background optical flow (the face masked out, so a nodding head is never mistaken for injection) correlated against device motion, lag-searched. A phone that never moves at all - a stand, an emulator - is rejected outright.

arXiv:2605.00218 - selfie-capture dynamics as an auxiliary signal
5

Light pulse - screen colour ↔ skin

The screen paints a server-minted, single-use colour sequence; the skin's reflected chroma must match it, timed within a lag window. A recording or a face-swap can't answer a challenge that didn't exist when the fake was made.

cf. iProov Flashmark - challenge–response liveness
6

Profile turn - the killer feature

A server-picked, per-session nonce: turn your head fully to one side. Real-time face-swap models map only 50–60% of landmarks in profile and visibly warp past ~60°. The side can't be pre-computed, and it's what trust professionals already ask for in a human video-ident call - legitimate, not exotic.

Metaphysic / Biometric Update - profile view breaks live face-swap
Simplified deliberately: an earlier version also matched the physical ID card in-frame. Card detection failed in real venue light on the first phone test, so we cut it - the head-turn signal alone was already validated and correct. Simpler and tested beats sophisticated and unverified.
Validation

How we'd know it works

Standard biometric anti-spoofing metrics, run against an attack kit built only on our own consenting faces:

APCER
Attack presentation classification error - % of virtual-cam / replay / stand attacks that get through
BPCER
Bona-fide presentation classification error - % of genuine users wrongly stepped up or blocked

Attack species mirror CEN/TS 18099: virtual camera, device/browser hooking, a phone on a stand. Every session - decision, per-signal scores, device, no images - lands in an audit log queryable live at GET /log, which is also the calibration tool for the thresholds below.

Data flywheel

What Swisscom's own data would train

confirmed fraud / genuine cases→ audit log→ per-signal score distributions→ calibrated thresholds→ tighter fusion

Today's thresholds are hand-set from synthetic tests and a handful of real runs. The log already stores exactly what's needed to replace them with fitted ones: face/motion/light/profile scores against the actual decision, first-name only, never an image, never a raw biometric trace.
